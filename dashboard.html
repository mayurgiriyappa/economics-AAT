Biopharma made in India, with a supply chain we control.

biopopcorn is an Indian biopharma company. A critical chromatography consumable in our purification process is imported, and rising cost, exchange-rate risk and long lead times have us asking one question: import or manufacture locally?

Our critical consumable: prepacked chromatography columns

Ready-to-use columns, already packed with chromatography resin and tested, that connect straight into a purification system.

What it is

A column body holding a uniformly packed resin bed, delivered qualified and ready to connect. Many are single-use.

Why it matters

It removes in-house packing time and bed-to-bed variability, and its performance affects the yield and purity of every batch.

Quality checks

Packed columns are typically tested for bed efficiency and peak symmetry before release.

45% Market Share

In terms of product, columns hold the highest (around 45%) share of the current market. Prepacked columns are widely used in Liquid Chromatography (LC).

What local manufacture requires

Localization is not just a price decision. Domestic production needs investment before it saves anything.

Packing tooling

Column hardware, slurry-packing and assembly equipment.

Validation

Proving packed-bed performance, such as efficiency and symmetry, in our process.

Supplier qualification

Auditing and approving resin, hardware, frit and sanitary-component suppliers.

Quality systems

Lot traceability, release testing and documentation for every packed column.

Show calculation formulas

Import unit cost = (USD purchase price × INR/USD + freight and insurance per unit) × (1 + effective non-recoverable import charges).

Local usable-unit variable cost = local attempted-unit production cost ÷ usable yield.

Local annual equivalent cost = annual demand × local usable-unit variable cost + annual quality/maintenance costs + initial localization investment ÷ evaluation horizon.

Break-even demand = (annual quality costs + initial investment ÷ horizon) ÷ (import unit cost − local usable-unit cost), where the denominator is positive.

Payback = initial investment ÷ [annual demand × (import unit cost − local usable-unit cost) − annual quality costs], where annual operating benefit is positive.

NPV = − initial investment + annual operating benefit × present-value annuity factor. Constant annual savings and end-of-year cash flows are assumed.

Yield is included once in local unit cost. GST is excluded only under the stated recoverable-input-tax assumption. No expected-disruption benefit is added.
